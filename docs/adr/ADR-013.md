# ADR-013: Append-only ledgers with derived balances and nightly proof

- Status: **Accepted**
- Decision area: financial and inventory integrity
- Related: [01 §7.3, §7.5, §10, §13](../01-system-architecture.md), [03 §5 F9, §6 M2/M6](../03-implementation-plan.md), [ADR-004](ADR-004.md), [ADR-006](ADR-006.md)

## Context

Inventory quantity, gift card balances, loyalty points, cash drawer totals and payment status are all derived from events that must be explainable years later. Mutable balance columns with a history table allow silent divergence and make tamper detection difficult.

## Decision

Every ledger (`inventory_movements`, payment events, loyalty/gift-card ledgers, cash movements, audit log) is append-only; a trigger forbids `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on movements and the application role has no such grant on the audit log. Balances (`inventory_balances`, `payment_intents.status`, `payment_status`) are projections updated in the same transaction as the fact. A **nightly proof job** recomputes projections from ledgers and raises `reconciliation_exceptions` on drift; it never silently overwrites. Proof jobs are numbered in the plan (1–2 inventory, 3, 4, 7, 8 reporting and sales, 5 cash). Reads of `rm_*` totals are proven against sums over `orders` and `payment_intents` per business date. The audit log additionally carries a per-tenant hash chain (`sha256(prev ‖ canonical(row))`) with the chain head anchored to S3 Object Lock.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Mutable balances plus a history table.** Rejected: nothing prevents the two disagreeing.
2. **Event sourcing everywhere.** Rejected: too heavy for entities where only the ledgers need history.

## Consequences

- Positive: tamper-evident money and stock; drift is an alert, not a silent fix; supports the hard invariant "acknowledged financial transaction loss = 0".
- Negative: corrections are new movements (reversal entries), which users must understand; ledgers grow, so partitioning and retention are needed.
- Gaps (see threat model): audit chain anchoring to S3 Object Lock and the nightly proof jobs were deferred in F1; until they exist, a privileged DB actor could rewrite the chain tail undetected (mitigation scheduled F9/F10).
